  # Guidebook for Past Tense - Igihe Cyashize mu Kinyarwanda

  ## Overview

  Welcome to the Past Tense lesson! In this unit, you'll learn how to talk about actions that have already happened in Kinyarwanda - from simple past statements to complete sentences about what you did yesterday. You'll master essential past tense verbs and conjugations for different subjects. With **500 XP** available, you'll be describing past events in Kinyarwanda with confidence!

  ## GRAMMAR TIPS

  ### Rule 1: Forming the Past Tense

  **Pattern:** [Subject prefix] + [past tense marker] + [verb stem]

  | Subject | Past Prefix | Verb (eat) | Past Form |
  |---------|-------------|------------|-----------|
  | I | **n-** | -rya | Nariye |
  | You | **w-** | -rya | Wariye |
  | He/She | **y-** | -rya | Yariye |
  | We | **tw-** | -rya | Twariye |
  | They | **b-** | -rya | Bariye |

  ### Rule 2: Simple Past Statements

  **Pattern:** [Subject + past verb] + [object/place]

  | Kinyarwanda | English |
  |-------------|---------|
  | Nariye ibiryo. | I ate food. |
  | Nanyoye amazi. | I drank water. |
  | Yagiye ku isoko. | He went to the market. |

  ### Rule 3: Past Tense with Objects

  **Pattern:** [Subject + past verb] + [object]

  | Kinyarwanda | English |
  |-------------|---------|
  | Naguriye igitabo. | I bought a book. |
  | Yabonye inzu nini. | He saw a big house. |

  ### Rule 4: Past Tense with People

  **Pattern:** [Subject + past verb] + [person]

  | Kinyarwanda | English |
  |-------------|---------|
  | Yahuye n'inshuti ye. | She met her friend. |
  | Bavuze muraho. | They said hello. |

  ### Rule 5: Past Tense with Adverbs

  **Pattern:** [Subject + past verb] + [adverb]

  | Kinyarwanda | English |
  |-------------|---------|
  | Yarahunyiriye neza. | He slept well. |
  | Yakoze neza. | He worked well. |

  ## SAMPLE DIALOGUES

  ### Dialogue 1: Talking About Yesterday

  A: Wakoze iki ejo?  
  (What did you do yesterday?)

  B: Nariye ibiryo. Nanyoye amazi.  
  (I ate food. I drank water.)

  A: Naguriye igitabo.  
  (I bought a book.)

  B: Ni byiza!  
  (That's good!)

  ### Dialogue 2: Where Did You Go?

  A: Yagiye he?  
  (Where did he go?)

  B: Yagiye ku isoko.  
  (He went to the market.)

  A: Yaguriye iki?  
  (What did he buy?)

  B: Yaguriye ibiryo.  
  (He bought food.)

  ### Dialogue 3: Meeting People

  A: Yahuye n'inshuti ye?  
  (Did she meet her friend?)

  B: Yego, yahuye n'inshuti ye.  
  (Yes, she met her friend.)

  A: Bavuze muraho?  
  (Did they say hello?)

  B: Yego, bavuze muraho.  
  (Yes, they said hello.)

  ### Dialogue 4: Children's Activities

  A: Abana bakoze iki?  
  (What did the children do?)

  B: Bakinnyije.  
  (They played.)

  A: Yarahunyiriye neza?  
  (Did he sleep well?)

  B: Yego, yarahunyiriye neza.  
  (Yes, he slept well.)

  ### Dialogue 5: What I Saw

  A: Wabonye iki?  
  (What did you see?)

  B: Nabonye imodoka nini.  
  (I saw a big car.)

  A: Waguriye iki?  
  (What did you buy?)

  B: Naguriye igitabo.  
  (I bought a book.)

  A: Ni byiza!  
  (That's good!)

  Master the past tense forms for "eat" with different subjects:
  - **Nariye** (I ate)

  - **Yariye** (He/She ate)
  - **Twariye** (We ate)
  - **Bariye** (They ate)

  - Nariye (I ate)
  - Nanyoye (I drank)

  ## FUN FACTS

  1. "Nariye" uses the prefix **"n-"** for "I" and the past marker **"-riye"** - the "iye" ending is a common past tense marker.
  2. "Yagiye" is one of the most common past tense verbs - you'll hear it often in everyday conversation.
  3. "Yanyoye" uses the same past marker **"-nyoye"** - many verbs follow this pattern.
  4. "Yarahunyiriye" is a longer past tense verb - it shows how adverbs can be combined with past tense.
  5. "Bakinnyije" has a double "n" - this is common in Kinyarwanda past tense forms.
  6. "Yavuze" is the past tense of **"kuvuga"** (to speak) - it's used for saying anything, not just "hello."
  7. "Yakoze" and **"yakoranye"** are both past tense forms of "to work" - they can be used interchangeably.
  8. "Yize" is the past tense of **"kwiga"** (to study/learn) - it's a short, common past tense verb.
